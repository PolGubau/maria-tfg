## Design Context

### Users
**Primary**: Psychology professionals and students who encounter this guide academically and share it with caregiving families. They arrive with critical, research-literate eyes - the design must signal credibility.  
**Secondary**: Family members of cancer patients reading during the oncological process, sometimes under acute emotional stress. They need calm, clarity, and a sense of being held - not overwhelmed by visual noise.

The dual audience is the core tension: *professional authority* for one group, *human warmth* for the other. The design must serve both without pandering to either.

### Brand Personality
**Three words: grounded, editorial, caring**

- **Grounded**: Rooted in real research with real people. No aspirational wellness-brand positivity.  
- **Editorial**: Curated, considered, typographically rigorous. Reads like a well-made book or a quality long-form publication, not a web app or hospital portal.  
- **Caring**: Genuinely human. Not clinical distance. Not saccharine warmth. The tone of a trusted professional who also happens to be kind.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Theme**: Light - caregivers arrive in daytime stress contexts; legibility and calm take priority.  
**Palette**: Warm neutrals with restrained editorial accents. Canvas `#fafaf7`, Ink `#1a1917`, accent terracotta `#8b6f5e`. Three phase hues (amber/teal/purple) used sparingly for orientation, never decoratively.  
**Typography**:
- Geist

**Anti-references** (what this must NOT look like):
- Hospital/clinic portals - sterile, system-font blues, institutional gray
- Cheerful mental health apps - illustrated characters, pastel gradients, patronizing
- Generic SaaS or nonprofit landing pages - hero + 3 icon cards + CTA
- Academic sites - walls of dense text with zero visual hierarchy

### Design Principles
1. **Typography IS the design** - spacing, scale, and typeface choices carry most of the aesthetic weight
2. **Whitespace signals respect** - generous margins communicate that the reader's attention is valued
3. **Phase identity through restraint** - phase colors orient, they don't decorate
4. **Progressive disclosure** - start simple, reveal complexity through interaction
5. **Every interactive element earns its place** - no component exists without a clear functional reason
